<section class="section">

## Restocking is the least profitable half of the working day

Every shop runs the same loop: sell, notice something is finishing, close up, travel to the market,
negotiate, carry the goods back, price them and shelve them. Repeated hundreds of times a year, that
loop is the least valuable half of an owner's week — and it fails in three predictable ways.

</section>

<div class="grid grid-3">
  <div class="card">
    <span class="card-number">1</span>
    <h3>You run out of what sells fastest</h3>
    <p>The items that leave the shelf quickest are the ones that go out of stock without warning, usually discovered after the day's sales are counted. That is lost revenue you cannot recover tomorrow.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="card">
    <span class="card-number">2</span>
    <h3>The market trip costs more than transport</h3>
    <p>There is the fare, and then there is the half-day or full day gone. For a small shop, closing the doors to go and buy stock is often the single largest cost of restocking.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="card">
    <span class="card-number">3</span>
    <h3>Cash sits in the wrong stock</h3>
    <p>Because a trip is expensive, owners buy big and buy rarely. That ties money up in slow-moving goods and creates dead stock and expiry losses.</p>
  </div>
</div>

## The loop we replace

Reorder is not an ordering app bolted onto a till. It runs one continuous loop, and every turn of
that loop ends with goods delivered and an order settled.

</section>

<div class="grid grid-3">
  <div class="card">
    <span class="card-number">1</span>
    <h3>Sell</h3>
    <p>You keep selling through the Reorder till or your own system. Sales are recorded as they happen.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="card">
    <span class="card-number">2</span>

    <p>Stock goes down automatically as items sell. Nothing to count by hand at the end of the day.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="card">
    <span class="card-number">3</span>

    <p>Reorder works out what is genuinely running low, using what actually sold, how long your supplier takes, and the pack sizes you buy in.</p>

  <div class="card">

  </div>
  <div class="card">
    <span class="card-number">5</span>
