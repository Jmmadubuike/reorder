# For suppliers, importers and manufacturers

If you sell fast-moving goods into Nigerian retail, your cost is not only the goods. It is the vans,
the sales reps, the small drop-offs, the returns and the credit you carry while chasing the long tail
of shops you cannot see into.

## What Reorder offers a supplier

| What you deal with today | What Reorder changes |
|---|---|
| Reps cold-calling small outlets for small orders | Orders arrive already sized, priced and batched by delivery route |
| No visibility of what a shop actually sold | Sell-through signal from shops that have agreed to share it |
| Guessing what to push and where | Demand that has already happened, not a forecast of it |
| Long-tail credit exposure | Settlement against delivered, confirmed orders |
| Short-dated and returned stock pushed to retailers | Agreed minimum remaining shelf life, so fewer disputes at the door |

## How orders reach you

1. A shop approves its restock list.
2. We confirm availability and price with you for the lines you supply.
3. You confirm, or you tell us what you cannot supply — and the shop is asked how to handle that line.
4. Goods are fulfilled either by your delivery or collected by our route, depending on the agreement.
5. Quantities are confirmed at delivery and settlement follows the terms we agreed.

## Working with us

Before any line goes live we agree, in writing:

- your price list, with pack definitions and minimum order quantities;
- your typical lead time, because it directly drives the restock calculation;
- what happens when a line is short, damaged or short-dated;
- who invoices whom, and on what payment terms;
- what customer information you see, if any.

## On customer information

Shop identities stay private by default. A supplier sees the demand lines they are being asked to
fulfil, not the shop's contact details, unless that shop has explicitly agreed to be identified. We
hold the restock relationship and the delivery route, and we would rather be explicit about that up
front than have it discovered later.

## What we are not asking for

We are not asking you to fund our growth, to extend credit you cannot control, or to hand over your
own customer relationships. We are asking for a predictable order flow in exchange for demand you
can plan production and delivery around.
