# REORDER

### Intelligent Restocking Infrastructure for Retail Businesses

**Proposed by:** Nexarch Technologies
**Product:** Reorder
**Initial Market:** Pharmacies, supermarkets, malls, convenience stores, shops and informal retailers
**Geographic Focus:** Nigeria, with expansion potential across Africa

---

# 1. Executive Summary

Reorder is a B2B restocking platform designed to eliminate the unnecessary stress, time, transportation cost and operational friction involved in replenishing products for retail businesses.

Today, many pharmacies, supermarkets, convenience stores, kiosks and informal retailers discover that products are exhausted only after completing their daily sales. The business owner or manager must then identify what is needed, travel to a market or distributor, negotiate with suppliers, purchase the products, transport them back and update their inventory.

Reorder changes this process.

Businesses use a simple POS/inventory system to record their sales. At the end of the business day, Reorder identifies products that need replenishment and turns those inventory needs into an actionable order.

Instead of the business owner going to the market, **the market comes to the business.**

Reorder aggregates demand from multiple businesses and sources products from manufacturers, importers, distributors and wholesalers. Products are then delivered directly to participating businesses.

For informal retailers that may not have the resources to purchase commercial POS software, Reorder provides a **free, lightweight retail management application**. These businesses receive useful digital tools at no software cost while Reorder becomes their procurement and replenishment channel.

The fundamental exchange is simple:

> **We give retailers the tools to know what they need. They give us the opportunity to supply what they need.**

# 2. The Problem

Retail businesses continuously experience the same operational problem:

**Products sell faster than they can be replenished.**

A typical retailer may finish a day knowing that several products have been exhausted or are approaching minimum stock levels.

The conventional process is:

1. Check what is missing.
2. Create a mental or handwritten shopping list.
3. Travel to a market or distributor.
4. Search for the products.
5. Compare prices.
6. Negotiate.
7. Purchase.
8. Arrange transportation.
9. Return to the business.
10. Receive and arrange the products.
11. Update inventory.

This creates several costs.

### Time Cost

Owners and employees spend hours travelling and searching for products instead of operating their businesses.

### Transportation Cost

Retailers incur transportation expenses simply to restock their businesses.

### Opportunity Cost

A business owner who spends four hours sourcing inventory is spending four hours away from sales, management and customers.

### Stockout Cost

When products are unavailable, the retailer loses sales and potentially loses customers.

### Procurement Inefficiency

Small retailers often purchase individually and therefore have weaker purchasing power than larger businesses.

### Information Gap

Many businesses do not know:

* which products are actually moving fastest;
* when they should reorder;
* how much they should reorder;
* where they can obtain better prices;
* which supplier is reliable;
* whether their current inventory is sufficient.

### Informal-Sector Challenge

A significant portion of Nigerian retail operates informally.

Many kiosks, roadside shops and small neighborhood retailers:

* do not use POS systems;
* do not maintain proper inventory records;
* do not have procurement systems;
* purchase from markets manually;
* operate with very limited working capital.

This creates both a problem and a large opportunity.

# 3. The Reorder Solution

Reorder combines **retail software, inventory intelligence, procurement and delivery** into one ecosystem.

The basic model is:

**SELL → TRACK → DETECT → REORDER → SOURCE → DELIVER**

A retailer sells products through the Reorder-powered POS.

The system continuously updates inventory.

When products reach their reorder threshold, the platform identifies them.

At the end of the day, the retailer receives a proposed replenishment list.

The retailer reviews and confirms the order.

Reorder aggregates the demand and sources the products from approved suppliers.

The products are delivered directly to the retailer.

The retailer therefore does not need to physically travel to the market for routine replenishment.

# 6. The Core Business Loop

The most important product loop is:

### Step 1 — Retailer sells

The retailer processes sales through Reorder's POS.

### Step 2 — Inventory changes

The system automatically deducts sold quantities.

### Step 3 — Reorder intelligence evaluates inventory

The system evaluates:

* current stock;
* minimum stock;
* reorder point;
* historical sales;
* sales velocity;
* previous orders;
* supplier availability.

### Step 4 — Reorder list is generated

The system proposes products that need replenishment.

### Step 5 — Retailer confirms

The retailer can:

* accept everything;
* remove items;
* change quantities;
* add additional products.

### Step 6 — Reorder aggregates demand

If 100 businesses require the same product, Reorder can aggregate that demand.

For example:

> 100 retailers × 20 units = 2,000 units.

Instead of 100 retailers individually visiting suppliers, Reorder potentially purchases the required quantity through a coordinated supply relationship.

### Step 7 — Supplier fulfills

The supplier provides the goods to Reorder or directly into the fulfillment network.

### Step 8 — Delivery

Reorder coordinates delivery to the retailers.

### Step 9 — Inventory updates

Delivered products are received into the retailer's inventory.

The cycle begins again.

# 11. Procurement Intelligence

The long-term system should move beyond simple stock thresholds.

The platform should eventually predict:

> "This product is likely to run out in approximately 4 days."

rather than merely:

> "Current stock is 5."

The system can consider:

**Average Daily Sales**

plus

**Supplier Lead Time**

plus

**Safety Stock**

to determine an appropriate reorder point.

A simplified model:

> Reorder Point = Average Daily Sales × Supplier Lead Time + Safety Stock

This becomes increasingly powerful as the platform accumulates transaction data.

Reorder becomes a bridge between **fragmented retail demand and concentrated supply.**

> **What was sold → What is running out → What needs to be purchased → Where it can be sourced → How it gets delivered.**

My recommendation for the next refinement

Before writing a single line of code, I would turn this into a proper Reorder Product Requirements & Business Architecture document with six additional sections:

Exact business model and unit economics — who pays, where margin comes from, delivery economics, supplier economics.
Detailed user roles — retailer, retailer staff, Reorder admin, procurement officer, supplier, distributor, manufacturer, driver.
End-to-end operational workflow — what happens from a ₦1,000 sale in a shop until the replacement stock arrives.
MVP functional requirements — every screen, action, permission and business rule.
Data architecture — products, SKUs, inventory, sales, reorder points, suppliers, purchase orders, deliveries, settlements, batches/expiry, etc.
Development roadmap — Phase 1 pilot → Phase 2 procurement network → Phase 3 demand intelligence → Phase 4 regional distribution.

The most important refinement is the economics. Reorder can have excellent software, but if procurement margin, delivery cost, minimum order quantities, supplier terms and retailer order frequency do not work together, the software alone will not make the business viable.

I would therefore do the business/economic model next, before the technical PRD.
